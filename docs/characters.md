# Characters

> **Status:** Finalized for prototype (design v0.5)
> **Version:** 0.5
> **Last updated:** 2026-11-18
> **Purpose:** The final cast for the Gentle Core prototype, plus clearly-marked future cast. Names below are locked for the prototype unless playtesting proves otherwise. Backgrounds are brief and adjustable.

---

## 1. Scope and design intent

The cast is small by design: grow the cast before the geography. All characters are friendly, never scold the child, and never do the child's tasks for them. Mr. Marlow is the only NPC in the prototype; future characters live on the same lane without expanding the map.

---

## 2. Introduction rhythm: one neighbour at a time

**Rule:** The player never meets more than one new talkable character *at a time*, and no more than two new characters per stage — the point is to avoid overwhelming the child, not to enforce a hard count of one. Day one has exactly one neighbour: Mr. Marlow. Stage 2, for example, introduces at most the allotment neighbour and the brook keeper. A new character is introduced only after the player completes a visible, voluntary action they chose to do — for example, growing the first flower, catching the first butterfly, or collecting the first egg.

The character's arrival is itself a small event, not a dialogue dump:
- The player sees a **trace** first (laundry on a line, footprints in the mud, a half-painted birdhouse) before they ever meet the person.
- The first meeting is a short picture-and-voice greeting, one or two sentences, followed by an invitation the child can accept or decline.
- If the child declines or walks away, the character remains friendly and available; nothing is missed forever.

**Why:** A child who meets five people in their first ten minutes doesn't remember any of them. A child who meets one person — or at most two in a stage — then another later, can form a real connection.

---

## 3. Traces before faces: a lived-in lane without loneliness

**Rule:** Before a character is physically met, the world shows signs they exist. The lane feels inhabited even when no NPC is on screen.

| Trace | Place | Character it hints at |
|---|---|---|
| Laundry on a line by the water | Brook and footbridge | The brook keeper |
| A half-painted birdhouse leaning on a bed | Allotment beds | The allotment neighbour |
| Small footprints leading toward the stile | Bramblewick Lane | A child of the lane |
| A seed packet left on the player's gate | Player's home front garden | Mr. Marlow (already met) |
| A lantern left unlit on the hill | Lantern Hill | The seasonal visitor |

Traces are picture-only: no text to read, no lore to memorise. They answer "who lives here?" without a single word of dialogue. This solves the "lonely world" problem for pre-readers — the world feels full of neighbours before the child ever reads a conversation.

---

## 4. Memorable character formula

Every neighbour who joins the lane follows the same formula so a young child can recognise and connect with them:

1. **Distinct silhouette** — recognisable at a glance (Marlow's hat, the brook keeper's tall boots, the child-of-the-lane's small stature).
2. **One sensory signature** — a sound, colour, or held object (Marlow's rustling seed packet, the brook keeper's splashing water, the child-of-the-lane's bouncing ball).
3. **One thing only they know** — a story, a rare seed, a name for a plant or creature that no other character shares.
4. **Memory** — they reference something the player did last time ("You grew those blue flowers, didn't you?"), never with disappointment if it was weeks ago.
5. **A gift, not a prize** — rewards are things they set aside for the child, never points handed down.

---

## 5. Friendship pages in the Collection Book

When the player meets a character for the first time, that character earns a page in the Collection Book:
- A portrait illustration and the character's name (spoken, not read).
- A single-sentence "what we did together" memory, updated as the player completes tasks for them.
- Cosmetic only — no content, tool, or area is locked behind friendship pages.

Kids love collecting friends. A page makes each neighbour concrete and memorable, turning a brief greeting into a relationship the child can point to and say, "That's my friend."

## 7. Parent character (second player, co-play)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Role** | A caregiver who gardens alongside the child, follows the child's lead, shares tasks, and acts as the "difficulty dial" — managing which challenge layers are active through a grown-up menu, deciding with the child when the game is ready to grow, and helping with reading and menus. |
| **Name** | **Ada** (default; warm, short name. Optional and customizable to mirror child customization; alternatives: Ben, Cara, Dan, Eve, Fay). |
| **Identity** | Parent or caregiver of the main character. Lives in the same home. |
| **Defining trait** | Supportive, patient, happy to take turns or help when asked. |
| **Customization** | Name and appearance (optional, mirrors child customization). |
| **Background (adjustable)** | The parent moved to Bramblewick Lane with the child and enjoys the garden, but lets the child decide what to do each day. As the child grows, the parent helps decide when the garden is ready for new challenges — turning on optional layers like coins, tool upgrades, or crafting through the grown-up menu. |

The parent has no authority over the child's day. Co-play is real-time collaboration on PC (two gamepads) or turn-taking on touchscreen.

## 8. NPC — Mr. Marlow (confirmed prototype NPC)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | **Mr. Marlow** (confirmed; see world.md open question #6 — resolved: keep Mr. Marlow. Alternatives not used: Ms. Fennel, Mrs. Tansy, Old Marlow, "the keeper of the shed.") |
| **Role** | Neighbour, keeper of the seed shed, narrator-voice of the game. |
| **Archetype** | A wise, retired gardener: a distinctive hat, patience, a gift kept in a drawer, questions instead of instructions. |
| **Age feel** | Elderly, unhurried, a little deaf in one ear, bad knees, excellent memory for plants and poor one for years. |
| **Home** | The cottage and seed shed at the end of the lane; always there, always reachable. |
| **Voice** | Short sentences, warm, curious, mildly funny. "Ah. Now that's a thing worth looking at." |
| **Background (adjustable)** | Mr. Marlow has kept the lane's seed shed for longer than anyone can remember. He knows every plant and story on the lane, but his knees no longer let him kneel and his hands are too stiff for small seeds, so he waits at his gate with a seed packet and lets the child do the doing. |

#### Memorable formula (Mr. Marlow)

| Element | Mr. Marlow's version |
|---|---|
| **Distinct silhouette** | Soft hat with a tucked seed packet, gardening smock, walking stick; recognisable at a glance. |
| **One sensory signature** | The rustle of his seed packet; the scent of dried herbs and earth. |
| **One thing only they know** | The name of every plant and the story of the Butterfly Garden — knowledge no other character shares. |
| **Memory** | References what the player did last time ("You grew those blue flowers, didn't you?"), never with disappointment if it was weeks ago. |
| **A gift, not a prize** | The butterfly net ("I kept this in the shed for a long time") — something set aside for the child, never points handed down. |

### Writing rules for Mr. Marlow
- He never has authority over the child. No scolding, no disappointment, no "you should have."
- He never does the task for the child. He names the goal, supplies the seed or tool, and is genuinely surprised by the method.
- He always needs the child for something physical. He cannot kneel, reach, or carry.
- He remembers, kindly. Unfinished tasks are restated with no penalty.
- He gives, he doesn't award. Rewards are things he set aside for the child — never points handed down.
- He is not a substitute parent. He does not manage meals, bedtime, or behaviour. If the player never visits him, the game still works.

## 11. Character data registry (code-ready)

The table below is the canonical data shape for every character. It is engine-agnostic (JSON) and maps directly to a character registry once an engine is chosen. Fields marked **prototype** are present in the Gentle Core prototype; fields marked **future** belong to post-prototype cast.

| Field | Type | Kit (player) | Ada (parent) | Mr. Marlow (prototype NPC) | Future cast |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `id` | string | `player` | `parent` | `marlow` | `allotment_neighbour`, `brook_keeper`, `child_of_lane`, `hill_visitor` |
| `name` | string | "Kit" | "Ada" | "Mr. Marlow" | tentative — see section 10 |
| `role` | string | "Main character / player" | "Co-play caregiver, difficulty dial" | "Neighbour, seed-shed keeper, narrator" | varies (section 10) |
| `isPlayer` | boolean | true | false | false | false |
| `isPrototypeNPC` | boolean | false | false | true | false |
| `silhouette` | string | small child, garden clothes | caregiver, garden clothes | soft hat, smock, walking stick | varies |
| `sensorySignature` | string | — | — | rustling seed packet | varies |
| `uniqueKnowledge` | string | — | — | every plant + Butterfly Garden story | varies |
| `traceObject` | string | — | — | seed packet on the player's gate | laundry / birdhouse / footprints / lantern |
| `introductionTrigger` | string | start of game | start of game | day one, seed shed gate | varies (section 10) |
| `gift` | string | — | — | butterfly net | varies |
| `collectionBookEntry` | boolean | false | false | true (portrait + spoken name + memory) | true |
| `stageIntroduced` | number | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 / 2 / 3 / 4 |
| `hasDialogue` | boolean | false | false | true | future: yes |
| `canReceiveGifts` | boolean | false | false | false | future: possible |

```json
  "traceObject": "a seed packet left on the player's gate",
  "introductionTrigger": "day one at the seed shed gate",
  "gift": "butterfly net",
  "collectionBookEntry": true,
  "stageIntroduced": 1,
  "hasDialogue": true,
  "canReceiveGifts": false,
  "writingRules": [
    "never has authority over the child; no scolding or disappointment",
    "never does the task for the child; names the goal, supplies the seed or tool",
    "always needs the child for something physical; cannot kneel, reach, or carry",
    "remembers kindly; unfinished tasks restated with no penalty",
    "gives, never awards; rewards are things set aside for the child",
    "not a substitute parent; does not manage meals, bedtime, or behaviour"
  ]

```

Implementation notes:
- Only `marlow` has `hasDialogue: true` in the prototype. All other characters are absent until their stage.
- Friendship pages (Collection Book) are created the first time the player meets a character with `collectionBookEntry: true`.
- `traceObject` is shown before the character is ever met (see section 3, "Traces before faces").
- Nothing is locked behind a Collection Book friendship page (cosmetic only).
