---

## 1. Scope and design intent

The cast grows with the farm: new faces arrive a few at a time as the farm reaches each tier. All characters are friendly, never scold Kit, and never do Kit's tasks for them. Mr. Marlow is the only NPC in the prototype; future characters live in **Bramblewick Vale**, most of them around the village green (places.md §2.1). The valley is fixed, so it fills with people rather than new places.

---

## 2. Introduction rhythm: one neighbour at a time

**Rule:** The player never meets more than one new talkable character *at a time*, and no more than **three** new characters per farm tier (progression.md §3; raised from two on 2026-11-20 for the bigger village). The travelling merchant is a visitor and does not count — the point is to avoid overwhelming the player, not to enforce a hard count of one. Day one has exactly one neighbour: Mr. Marlow. **Owner decision 2026-11-21:** the baker, the allotment neighbour and the shopkeeper are introduced during **Tier 1** through orders pinned on Marlow's board (one at a time, each after a visible action of Kit's), which uses Tier 1's three slots; Tier 2 then opens their buildings. A new character is introduced only after the player completes a visible, voluntary action — for example, selling the first crop, collecting the first egg, or visiting the brook.

The character's arrival is itself a small event, not a dialogue dump:
- The player sees a **trace** first (laundry on a line, footprints in the mud, a half-painted birdhouse) before they ever meet the person.
- The first meeting is a short picture-and-voice greeting, one or two sentences, followed by an invitation the player can accept or decline.
- If the player declines or walks away, the character remains friendly and available; nothing is missed forever.

**Why:** A player who meets five people in their first ten minutes doesn't remember any of them. A player who meets one person — or at most two in a tier — then another later, can form a real connection (and learn each neighbour's order tastes).

## 3. Traces before faces: a lived-in lane without loneliness

**Rule:** Before a character is physically met, the world shows signs they exist. The lane feels inhabited even when no NPC is on screen.

| Trace | Place | Character it hints at |
|---|---|---|
| Laundry on a line by the water | Brook and footbridge | The brook keeper |
| A half-painted birdhouse leaning on a bed | Allotment beds | The allotment neighbour |
| Small footprints leading toward the stile | Bramblewick Lane | A friend of Kit's age |
| A seed packet left on the player's gate | Player's home front garden | Mr. Marlow (already met) |
| A lantern left unlit on the hill | Lantern Hill | The seasonal visitor |
| A delivery crate outside the shop door | General store | The shopkeeper |
| A horseshoe hung on the smithy door; a hammer's ring | Smithy | The smith |
| Herb pots on the clinic windowsill | Clinic | The village nurse |
| Sawdust and a half-built bench in the yard | Carpenter's yard | The carpenter |
| A stack of picture books on the bench by the green | Library | The librarian |
| Fresh lanterns being hung over the inn door | Inn | The innkeeper |

Traces are picture-only: no text to read, no lore to memorise. They answer "who lives here?" without a single word of dialogue. The world feels full of neighbours before the player ever reads a conversation.

## 4. Memorable character formula

Every neighbour who joins the lane follows the same formula so the player can recognise and connect with them:

1. **Distinct silhouette** — recognisable at a glance (Marlow's hat, the brook keeper's tall boots, the friend's bright scarf).
2. **One sensory signature** — a sound, colour, or held object (Marlow's rustling seed packet, the brook keeper's splashing water, the friend's bicycle bell).
3. **One thing only they know** — a story, a rare seed, a name for a plant or creature that no other character shares.
4. **Memory** — they reference something the player did last time ("You grew those blue flowers, didn't you?"), never with disappointment if it was weeks ago.
5. **A gift, not a prize** — rewards are things they set aside for Kit, never points handed down.

---

## 5. Friendship pages in the Collection Book

When the player meets a character for the first time, that character earns a page in the Collection Book:
- A portrait illustration and the character's name (spoken, not read).
- A single-sentence "what we did together" memory, updated as the player completes tasks for them.
- Cosmetic only — no content, tool, or area is locked behind friendship pages.

A page makes each neighbour concrete and memorable, turning a brief greeting into a relationship the player can point to and say, "That's my friend."

## 8. NPC — Mr. Marlow (confirmed prototype NPC)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | **Mr. Marlow** (confirmed; see world.md open question #6 — resolved: keep Mr. Marlow. Alternatives not used: Ms. Fennel, Mrs. Tansy, Old Marlow, "the keeper of the shed.") |
| **Role** | Neighbour, keeper of the seed shed, narrator-voice of the game. |
| **Archetype** | A wise, retired gardener: a distinctive hat, patience, a gift kept in a drawer, questions instead of instructions. |
| **Age feel** | Elderly, unhurried, a little deaf in one ear, bad knees, excellent memory for plants and poor one for years. |
| **Home** | The cottage and seed shed on the lane, between Kit's gate and the village green; always there, always reachable. |
| **Voice** | Short sentences, warm, curious, mildly funny. "Ah. Now that's a thing worth looking at." |
| **Background (adjustable)** | Mr. Marlow has kept the lane's seed shed for longer than anyone can remember. He knows every plant and story on the lane, but his knees no longer let him kneel and his hands are too stiff for small seeds, so he waits at his gate with a seed packet and lets Kit do the doing. He also keeps the lane's **order board** at the seed shed and sells seeds, and he likes herbs and honey in his own orders. |

#### Memorable formula (Mr. Marlow)

| Element | Mr. Marlow's version |
|---|---|
| **Distinct silhouette** | Soft hat with a tucked seed packet, gardening smock, walking stick; recognisable at a glance. |
| **One sensory signature** | The rustle of his seed packet; the scent of dried herbs and earth. |
| **One thing only they know** | The name of every plant and the story of the old butterfly meadow — knowledge no other character shares. |
| **Memory** | References what the player did last time ("You grew those blue flowers, didn't you?"), never with disappointment if it was weeks ago. |
| **A gift, not a prize** | The first order and the order board ("Your folk sold me berries every summer") — later the butterfly net, "kept in the shed for a long time". Never points handed down. |

### Writing rules for Mr. Marlow
- He never has authority over Kit. No scolding, no disappointment, no "you should have."
- He never does the task for Kit. He names the goal, supplies the seed or tool, and is genuinely surprised by the method.
- He always needs Kit for something physical. He cannot kneel, reach, or carry.
- He remembers, kindly. Unfinished tasks are restated with no penalty.
- He gives, he doesn't award. Rewards are things he set aside for Kit — never points handed down.
- He is not a substitute parent. He does not manage Kit's day. If the player never visits him, the game still works.

**Knowledge layers (decided — world.md §7.3, "a+")**

| Layer | Content | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · What | Goal, reward, next step | **Always says it**, one picture + one sentence. Never hides the want |
| 2 · Where / who | "The beds by the gate" | **Always points**, never fetches |
| 3 · How | The method (which tool, what order) | **Never says it first** — only on request or after the hint ladder |
| 4 · Why | The lane's memory, why anything works | **Never** — max two allusions per playthrough, as shrugs (world.md §4.1) |
| 5 · Doing | The action itself | **Never.** And he never judges the result |

**Invitation, not instruction:** everything about what happens next is offered ("Would you like to plant them now, or shall we look at the lane first?"); declining is always free. In co-play he **addresses the pair**, so both players decide out loud.

**Hint ladder (just-in-time, escalating, no failure state):**

1. **0 s** — Kit acts → world feedback (sparkle, sound, benign puff if the tool is wrong)
2. **~15 s stuck** — picture bubble *points* at the object (icon only, no words)
3. **Still stuck** — leading question: *"Shall we try the seed box?"*
4. **Still stuck** — plain instruction: *"Tap the bed with the seed box."*

The ~15 s window is deliberate: **in co-play the second player speaks first** ("which tool plants things?") and Marlow stays quiet; in solo play the ladder is the fallback. One behaviour, two audiences. The ladder applies to tutorials and first-time actions; once a verb is known, Marlow stays quiet unless asked. Never: a wrong hint, a countdown, a buzzer, or a scolding.

**Allowed wrongness — (a+):** he may be wrong only about **flavour** (years, stories, which flower is which), corrected **by the world on screen in the same beat** and laughed off (*"No — Lace Flower! Ha — these old eyes. Your eyes are better than mine, you know."*). **Actionable knowledge is never wrong** — Kit can never follow him into a dead end.
