# Progression: Farm Growth, Automation, and Long-Term Goals

> **Status:** Design draft — **restructured 2026-11-19**
> **Version:** 0.11
> **Last updated:** 2026-11-20
> **Purpose:** How the farm grows from a tangled plot to a full, automated farm (*Harvest Moon*-style), what limits it at each point, and what there is to chase once it runs well. Replaces the old parent-controlled layers and five-stage age map. Everything after the core farm loop is **optional**.

---

## 1. Design Philosophy

- **Challenge rises naturally as the farm grows.** More beds mean more watering; more animals mean more feeding; bigger orders mean more planning. Nothing is toggled by a parent and nothing is gated by age.
- **Early limit: money. Then cleared land. Final limit: total land.** All 992 tiles of land are Kit's from day one (buildings.md §2), but most is overgrown. Kit clears it at their own pace — or never. Later, money is plentiful and the question becomes *what fits*.
- **Automation is the turning point** (GDD pillar 5): it arrives when the farm has become too big to tend by hand, so it feels earned and relieving.
- **Nothing is mandatory.** Orders, contests, collections, and decorations are invitations. A player can keep a small, hand-tended farm forever.
- There is one difficulty mode — realistic but kid-friendly (challenge-layers.md).

## 3. Farm Growth Tiers

Tiers are reached by **what the farm is**, not by time played or age. They gate the building catalogue (buildings.md) and order sizes.

| Tier | Reached when | Typical limit | What opens | Order size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 — Small plot** | Start of game | **Money** (500 coins, 3 cleared beds) | Seeds, clearing the front garden, compost bin, **5 hens + 1 cow already in the old coop and barn**, feed, the order board (first order on Day 1) | 1–6 items |
| **T2 — Growing farm** | First 10 orders delivered | **Money** | Big coop, beehive, workshop, kitchen (processing), market stall, **farmhands hired by the day**, first blueprints, scythe/hammer/axe, brush, fishing rod | 3–10 items, mixed |
| **T3 — Working farm** | First back field (Field A) fully cleared | **Money + time** (hand-tending and clearing get busy) | Cows and sheep for the **old starting barn**, extra/bigger barns, silo, fish pond, orchard, travelling merchant visits, festivals & contests | Baskets, weekly repeats |
| **T4 — Automated farm** | First sprinkler or auto-feeder placed | **Land** begins to bite | Sprinklers (all grades), auto-feeders, pond auto-feeder, greenhouse, garden sprites | Bulk orders, catering |
| **T5 — Full land** | All five back fields cleared | **Land only** | Hydroponics, farm shop, lane restoration, endgame goals (§6) | Standing orders, festival catering |

**Where the valley opens** (places.md §2): **T1** the farm, the lane, the village green, the brook's near bank · **T2** Hazel Wood (Kit's first axe clears the fallen log), bakery, general store, village hall, the villagers' cottages (farmhands) · **T3** footbridge → far bank and Mill Pond (restoration step 1), smithy, friend's cottage · **T4** watermill (step 2), clinic, carpenter's yard, library · **T5** stile → Lantern Hill and the Old Quarry (step 3), inn.

**Tools across tiers:** watering can (start) → bigger can, hoe, scythe (T2) → brush, fishing rod (T2) → upgraded tools (T3+, at the village smithy). Tool upgrades cost money and materials; old tools never disappear.

## 4. Land: All Yours, but Overgrown

- **All land is unlocked from the start** (owner decision 2026-11-19): the front garden and Fields A–E behind the house, 992 tiles in total (buildings.md §2). Nothing is bought.
- **It must be cleared before use.** Each field is covered in debris that gets harder further back:

| Debris | Cleared with | Gives | Found in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weeds, tall grass | Hands / scythe | Fibre | Everywhere |
| Small stones | Hammer | Stone | Front garden, Fields A–B |
| Stumps | Axe | Wood | Fields B–C |
| Big stones, logs | Upgraded hammer / axe | Stone, wood | Fields D–E |
| Boulders, big logs | Best hammer / axe | Lots of stone, wood | Field E |

- **No stamina, no deadline.** Clearing is just time: each swing takes a moment, and a field takes a few in-game days of steady work. Players can clear a corner, a field, or nothing — the farm works at any size.
- Better tools (money) open the harder fields, so **money still gates land early**; clearing time gates it in the middle; **total land** is the final limit.
- Cleared materials (wood, stone, fibre) feed the workshop, so clearing pays for itself.
- The valley around the farm is fixed (places.md §1); only what Kit clears and builds on the farm changes.

## 5. Neighbour Orders as Progression

| Stage | Example | What it practises |
|---|---|---|
| Single item | "Six eggs, please." | Producing to a request |
| Mixed | "Two carrots, a jar of honey." | Planning several products |
| Processed | "An apple pie for Sunday." | Processing chains |
| Seasonal basket | "A winter basket: herbs, cheese, jam." | Seasons, storage, greenhouse |
| Bulk | "Forty eggs for the festival." | Scaling up, automation |
| Repeat / standing | "A basket every week." | Reliable supply, relationships |
| **Subscription (daily)** | "6 eggs every morning by 10:00." Reminder each day; cancel any time | Routine, reliability, steady income |

Orders always match what Kit can produce right now (GDD §4) and never carry a deadline penalty.

## 6. After the Farm Is Running — Long-Term Goals (all optional)

When the land is full and automation runs the chores, there is still plenty to chase. None of it is required.

### 6.1 Decoration and farm charm

- **Decorations:** fences, paths, flower borders, lanterns, statues, benches, scarecrows, seasonal ornaments, signposts, a painted door for the house.
- **Get them by:** buying (shops, travelling merchants), crafting (workshop blueprints), or **winning** them (contests, friendship gifts, collections).
- **Place anywhere** on the farm; move freely.
- **Farm charm:** a visible charm rating (shown as a row of flowers, not a number) that rises with decorations, tidy paths, flower variety, and well-kept animals.
  - **Why it matters:** higher charm brings **more visitors to the farm shop and market stall**, unlocks **special neighbour visits and photo moments**, and counts in the **"Prettiest Farm" festival contest**.
  - **Trade-off:** decorations use land that could grow crops — beauty versus production is a choice, not a rule.

### 6.2 Collections

| Collection | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Rare crops** | Golden pumpkin, giant variants, seeds only from merchants or contests |
| **Fish** | Every pond and brook species, including rare seasonal fish |
| **Animal breeds** | Speckled hens, black sheep, highland cow, rare bee strains — bought from merchants or won |
| **Recipes** | Every jam, pie, bread, cheese, and preserve |
| **Forage finds** | Wild herbs, mushrooms, shells, fossils from exploring |

Collections fill the **Collection Book** (inventory.md §6) and can be shown in the showcase (§6.5).

### 6.3 Festival and contest trophies

Seasonal festivals hold contests — biggest pumpkin, best eggs, finest wool, best jam, prettiest farm, biggest fish. Ribbons and trophies are displayed on the farm; some contests award unique decorations or rare seeds.

### 6.4 Restoring the lane

The lane's long-arc story (world.md §4.3), now spread across the late game as a **ladder of three community projects** (2026-11-20, Bramblewick Vale). Each is planned at the village hall, has its own order list at Marlow's board (money and produce, plus materials from clearing), and visibly changes the valley by opening an area (places.md §2):

| Step | Tier | Project | Opens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | T3 | **Repair the footbridge** | The brook's far bank: butterfly meadow (replant it as part of the step), the brook keeper's cottage, Mill Pond |
| 2 | T4 | **Restore the old watermill** | The mill turns and grinds flour (a new processing chain for the baker's orders) |
| 3 | T5 | **Rebuild the stile, then relight the lantern** | Lantern Hill, the lookout, and the Old Quarry; relighting happens at Winter Lantern Night |

All three are optional; the farm never depends on them.

### 6.5 Showcase / farm museum

A small showcase building (or a room in the house) that displays collections, trophies, rare items, and recipes. Visitors comment on it; a full showcase is its own achievement.

### 6.6 Mastery goals

Per-activity mastery tracks (farming, animal care, fishing, cooking, building) with simple milestones ("grow every crop", "raise a 3-star product of every animal"). Each mastery level unlocks a cosmetic reward or rare blueprint.

### 6.7 Prestige rare items (optional)

Very rare items for players who want them: golden watering can, prize-winning breeds, a weathervane for the barn, legendary fish. Purely cosmetic or slightly better — never needed for anything else.

## 8. Seasonal Mysteries

Optional observation goals that recur every year:

| Season | Mystery | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| **Spring** | "Where did the butterflies go?" (the Butterfly Garden request) | Butterfly net |
| **Summer** | "The beehive is glowing at night." | Rare bee strain |
| **Autumn** | "The sheep's wool is extra thick." | Quality wool bundle |
| **Winter** | "Marlow's lantern is dark." | Garden lantern (tool) — distinct from the unlit lantern on Lantern Hill |

Missing a mystery means waiting for it to return next year.
